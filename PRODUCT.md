# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Enterprise hiring managers, CTOs, and heads of infrastructure at large organisations evaluating whether to engage Väinö for consulting, contracting, or a senior architecture role. They arrive with a specific question: "Can this person handle the complexity of our environment?" They are technically literate — many are architects themselves — and will spot vague language or surface-level claims immediately. They scan quickly; the page must establish seniority within 10 seconds.

## Product Purpose

rikkova.fi is Väinö Rikkonen's professional portfolio and primary inbound channel. It exists to convert a technical visitor's initial scepticism into a credible first impression that leads to a conversation. Success looks like a hiring manager or CTO reaching out rather than closing the tab. The page is the pitch.

## Brand Personality

Precise. Authoritative. Honest about tradeoffs.

The brand voice is the voice of someone who has shipped production infrastructure at scale and has the scar tissue to prove it. It doesn't oversell, it doesn't hedge, and it does not perform expertise — it demonstrates it through specific, measurable outcomes and principled language. The emotional goal is *earned confidence*, not enthusiasm.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS / Stripe-clone aesthetic: purple or teal gradients, frosted-glass cards, cream or neutral-white body, Inter or Plus Jakarta Sans, the standard dev-tool landing page template. This aesthetic signals "AI-generated portfolio" and undercuts the technical credibility the site is trying to establish.
- Bland corporate consultant site: LinkedIn-in-webpage format, stock-photo backgrounds, corporate blue/grey palette, bullet-point service lists without proof.

## Design Principles

1. **Proof over claim.** Every statement of expertise should be adjacent to a specific, verifiable outcome. Numbers, protocol names, scope. Not "experienced in networking" — "VXLAN-EVPN fabric migration, zero service interruption."
2. **Designed for the day-two reader.** The page should be as understandable on a second scan — from a different angle, a different time — as on the first. Information architecture must hold up, not just impress on first load.
3. **System coherence.** The visual language should feel like it was designed by someone who designs systems: consistent, documented, honest about what each element is doing and why. No decorative complexity.
4. **Earn the motion.** Every animation should enhance what it reveals, not substitute for content. Motion that could be removed without loss should be removed.
5. **Terminal register, not terminal cosplay.** The technical aesthetic earns its place because the work is technical. It should feel like a well-maintained runbook, not a toy CLI simulator.
